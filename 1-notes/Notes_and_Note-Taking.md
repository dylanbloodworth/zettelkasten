Why take notes:
1. Capture fleeting ideas
2. Actively engage with content (e.g. literature, lectures, etc.)
3. Synthesize ideas into trains of thought


## Capture Fleeting Thoughts

I'll have ideas and thoughts randomly pop into my head at inopportune times, and these thoughts will escape me in less than a minute. Notes can be a very useful tool to archive these "fleeting" thoughts I have throughout the day.

>While humans excel at generating insights, our capacity to retain them is limited. We’re forgetful. We lose our train of thought.
>-[Doto](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/R6EZAKMC?page=null&annotation=CJLML5UA)

Fleeting notes aren't useful on their own. They're a net for catching ideas; it's a reminder that you once had a curiosity about something you saw, read, said, or experienced that made an impact.

>It's probably more important to reflect rather than collect
>-Treasury (pg 27)

## Engage with Content

Under the framework of active learning, learners are responsible for constructing meaning and knowledge from the stimulus they interact with.
- [[4.1a Active engagement's key in the learning process to generate knowledge.]]

While reading, reference notes and the note-taking process should focus on the active construction of knowledge. It seems important to note and engage with your thoughts on the material, as opposed to mindless copying.

> Anything that shows up while reading—external references, feelings, past and current events, a conversation you had earlier in the day—anything that gives you insight into what you think about what you’re encountering is grist for the writing mill. 
>-[Doto](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/R6EZAKMC?page=null&annotation=CJLML5UA)

> Reading is not note-taking, and you should feel free to cite anything that catches your attention, regardless if it will eventually make it into your slip box.
>-[Doto](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/R6EZAKMC?page=null&annotation=CJLML5UA)

Providing citations or references (lectures, books, conversations, journals) can help to put the ideas in reference notes into context.
